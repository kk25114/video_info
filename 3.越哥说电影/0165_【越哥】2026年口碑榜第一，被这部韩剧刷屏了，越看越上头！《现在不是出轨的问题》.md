It’s not just about an affair; this is a 2026 series directed by Lee Chang-hee, consisting of eight episodes and starring Kim Hye-soo, Jo Jung-suk, and Kim Ji-hoon. To be honest, seeing the title, I expected another melodramatic marriage soap opera, but I soon realized the drama was just wrapping paper for a dark comedy, telling the same twisted joke from start to finish. Every time they think the worst is over, something even worse knocks at their door the very next second. Getting caught for an affair, being blackmailed, burying a body, and paying a ransom only to receive their husband’s cheating video; the daughter they tried so hard to hide never even drove the car, and the two children they labeled as murderers didn't actually kill anyone. You'll notice there are no purely evil people here, nor are there any truly good ones; there are only ordinary people desperately trying to maintain their dignity. Some want to keep the image of a captain, some want to save a husband’s reputation, and others want to keep custody of their daughter. To maintain this facade, they tell one lie, only to be forced to tell ten more to cover it up. Ultimately, what they fear isn't doing wrong, but getting caught doing wrong—that is the darkest part of this show. I think the cruelest stroke is using the children as a mirror: adults scold kids for not understanding love, then cheat themselves; adults teach kids to be honest, then bury a body themselves. The kids taking the fall for them and the parents burying a body for the kids share the same twisted logic: that loving someone means hiding their mistakes. We may have many such families around us; always harmonious on the outside, but inside, no one dares to speak, because the moment someone does, the image of their perfect family shatters. In the first episode, the couple tells the camera, "We will try our best to live well." By the final episode, they really are living "well"—only they’re standing on top of the concrete in the cellar.

Perhaps every seemingly happy family has a cellar that cannot be opened, and truly living well isn't about sealing problems in concrete, but having the courage to face them while they are still small.
