“The Spider-Man” is a 2026 Netflix series directed by Kim Da-min, which earned a perfect score on Rotten Tomatoes, though only from 7 critics; the audience rating is 73%. Honestly, that score is quite accurate; the parts that are good are very good, but its flaws are also very obvious. First, the good: the show’s premise is a workplace-themed episodic horror—convenience store, delivery, cinema, hospital, construction site, amusement park—each job has a monster and a set of rules, with the hourly wage doubling, and the audience follows Gyeong-jun through the stages, the pacing pushing forward, but what’s truly scary…

What’s scary isn’t what’s on the other side of the door. Hospitals use people for organ harvesting, construction sites use them for foundation, and the ones doing these things are living people. There’s a line in the show saying the other world is no different, because everything comes from this world; the monsters on the other side were created here first. I also liked the subplot where Gyeong-il was a textile factory 40 years ago, then an amusement park, and finally a conglomerate. The slogans on the walls changed from “Faster, More, Cheaper” to “50x Wage,” but it’s just changing the packaging. The boss’s theory about sacrificing the world sounds mysterious, but it’s essentially making the poorest people take on the most dangerous jobs. As for the flaws, 8 episodes have to cover 6 jobs, plus a search subplot, so characters just rush through; some say they didn’t really get to know them after watching. Byeong-seok was just starting to feel sympathetic before he disappeared; the later monsters rely more on CGI, and I feel the creepiness isn’t as effective as that simple note in the convenience store in the 1st episode. The father-son reunion was built up for all 8 episodes, but they were only together for a few minutes. The father’s generation believed in superstitions, while Gyeong-jun’s generation believes in a 50x wage, yet in this show, every high-paying gig is waiting for someone who can never go home. So, there’s no such thing as fortune-telling in this world; getting off work safely is all that matters.
