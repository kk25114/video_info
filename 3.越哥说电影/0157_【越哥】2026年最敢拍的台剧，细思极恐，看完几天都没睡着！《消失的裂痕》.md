## 简介

故事围绕因儿子失踪而陷入抑郁的母亲展开：镜子裂开后，她发现镜中有一个雾霾笼罩、贫穷压抑的平行世界，两个世界相貌相同的人发生身份互换。丈夫被调包、家庭秘密与婚外情接连浮现，富裕与贫困、温情与暴力互为镜像。作品以悬疑外壳探讨丧子创伤、婚姻背叛、阶层差异和人性贪欲，最终指向真正需修补的不是镜面裂缝，而是人不敢直视的内心裂痕。

This is undoubtedly the boldest Taiwanese drama of the year. Within just 30 minutes of release, it became a massive hit across Taiwan. It's only 6 episodes long, and the main cast members almost all play dual roles. On this side of the mirror, there are mansions, luxury cars, and a respectable family worth hundreds of millions; on the other side, there is a gray world shrouded in perpetual smog where you must wear a mask to step outside. The same faces, the same home, yet 2 lives that are completely opposite.

Have you ever thought that the life you are living now might not be your own? The people around you might not be the people you remember. Your friends, your family, your partner, and your world have long been quietly replaced, and it all stems from...

Late at night, Yun stands in the center of the living room, her gaze fixed on the lampshade overhead. Inside the lampshade hides—she frantically ransacks the room—the bookshelf, the corners of the walls, behind the photos. 1, 2, 3—this home had long been filled with eyes watching her. The lock turns; her husband has returned. He glances at the pile of cameras on the table, asks nothing, and instead talks to her as gently as he always does.

Yun tentatively brings up a reading game—a little secret known only to her and her missing son. Her husband smiles and continues it, getting every detail right about where the boy had made a mistake back then. At that moment, she almost believed she was losing her mind, until her husband raised his hand, revealing a few dark red spots on his arm. She had seen these red spots before. In another world, in a city shrouded in smog, the man before her who looks exactly like her husband is not her husband at all.

She excuses herself to the bathroom and locks the door. The footsteps outside draw closer, step by step. She endures the wait inside until dark, then finally dares to push the door open a crack. The man on the bed seems to be asleep, but just as she tiptoes past—the person sharing her bed had been quietly swapped. The real husband is currently trapped on the other side of a forbidden zone, his fate unknown.

This absurd and terrifying identity theft goes back 3 years. That day, Yun was hiking with her son, Xiao Yu, in the mountains of Hsinchu. Xiao Yu was born with autism; he didn't like to talk or respond, only focused on walking ahead by himself. Halfway up the mountain, a thunderclap split the sky. Xiao Yu was so frightened he crouched on the ground. When he looked up, he saw something perched on a tree branch—

"Yellow bird, yellow bird, yellow bird, yellow bird."

"Baby, there are no yellow birds in this season."

He raised his phone to take a picture, but the butterfly flew into a nearby pavilion. He aimed his lens at it again, but the screen showed nothing; it had clearly just landed.

"I went in."

The mother caught up, the clouds gathered, and he turned to take his son into the pavilion to hide for a moment, but... Baby!

3 days and 3 nights of searching yielded nothing, and the rescue was called off a week later. A child vanished into thin air in broad daylight from behind his mother, leaving only 2 cold words in the case file: missing.

3 years have passed, and Yun still hasn't been able to walk out of that pavilion. She developed severe depression, often hearing her son's voice in the morning, only to turn around and find the house empty. Waking up this day, she saw something land on the roof... Opening her eyes again, everything was as usual. She could no longer tell what was real and what was just her illness. While bathing, wind picked up outside, the windmill in the yard began spinning in reverse, and the mirror fogged up in the bathroom... Yet, lost in the water, she was completely unaware.

In a flowerpot on the balcony, a butterfly had just emerged. Yun put it in a box, drove back to that mountain, and opened the box by the pavilion, watching it fly away, as if doing so might help her son find his way back to her. She knew it was just self-deception, but for these 3 years, it was all she had left to cling to.

Before marriage, she was a well-known painter; the home studio was Xiao Yu's favorite place to be after school. Her husband, Lao He, wanted to sell it so she could recover in peace, but she held onto it, fearing that if her son came back one day, he wouldn't have a place to paint.

"Oh, the clients just now really liked the location and the lighting here, especially how clean this space is kept."

"My son has severe allergies. This place must be kept spotlessly clean. Otherwise, he would start sneezing the moment he walks in. Every time he got out of school, he would come straight here to paint. And then go home after he was done."

Because of this delay, she forgot to pick up their daughter. Lao He brought their daughter home, suppressed his anger the whole way, but finally let it out; the couple started arguing in the living room. Their daughter went to her room, and Lao He calmed down and went to apologize. Yun didn't blame him; she was just trapped in self-reproach, telling herself over and over that Xiao Yu was still alive.

In the study, Lao He watched home videos from before his son went missing. Back then, all the family's love went to this special child; now, the laughter in the footage sounded more grating with every passing moment. Like his wife, he was also trapped in that time 3 years ago, but as the pillar of the family, he couldn't collapse. He forced himself to stay clear-headed, never realizing that his wife was slowly sinking away.

Late at night, a child's laughter rings out again from the locked storage room. Sister Yun goes downstairs to find the key, waking Lao He.

"What are you doing? Why aren't you sleeping?"

"I heard it. It sounded like a child laughing and footsteps. Did you hear it?"

"No."

"How is that possible? It was so clear, how could you not hear it? Why are you still up so late? Can't you sleep? Here, have some milk to drink."

Lao He doesn't turn around; he just asks if she has taken her medication today. Sister Yun insists she isn't sick. That night, Lao He resolves that he must get his wife to undergo treatment.

The next day, Lao He takes their daughter to her new school in Taipei, leaving only Sister Yun in the empty house. She uses a towel to wipe a crack. The reflection in the mirror does not move with her. She flees the bathroom and dials her husband's number, but when she looks back into the mirror, everything appears normal. The words catch in her throat, and she swallows them; she doesn't want to be the patient who makes the whole family worry again. As she turns to leave, she doesn't see the crack.

At the hospital, Sister Yun tells her attending physician, Dr. An, that her hallucinations are becoming more frequent. Dr. An asks if she has been troubled by anything lately. She thinks of her daughter, of the old house her husband insists on selling, and breaks down in tears as she speaks. Dr. An has seen too many patients like her, trapped forever in the past by a single day or a single event. He prescribes some sedatives, but he knows in his heart that the real medicine lies with her family.

Meanwhile, Lao He asks his brother, Lao Sun, to sell the house, but it has been listed for a long time without a buyer. It's not that the house is hard to sell; it's that Lao Sun, out of consideration for his sister-in-law, hasn't dared to truly sell it. It's not that Lao He doesn't understand his wife's attachment, but he can no longer bear the strain of traveling between Taipei and their hometown.

The next day, Lao Sun comes over to take photos and freezes when he pushes open the bathroom door. Every mirror in the house is covered with cloth. Afraid to see what's inside, she has shut all the mirrors away in darkness.

At night, a sound comes from next door again. "Hi."

Behind the door is a strange girl who says she is a classmate of their daughter, Xiao Ye, here to return headphones. The giggling sounds from the previous nights were just the 2 of them; Xiao Ye didn't dare say anything for fear of disturbing her mother. So, those sounds that kept her up all night were nothing more than the whispers of 2 girls.

With the truth being so ordinary, Sister Yun feels a sense of loss. She pulls the cloth off the mirror, brings a chair to sit in front of it, and waits to see if the hallucination will appear. As she waits, she falls asleep. The moment she closes her eyes, a crack appears. On the other side of the glass, Xiao Yu, who has been missing for 3 years, is standing at the vanity, looking at his mother in the mirror. Staring.

Past 2 in the morning, Yun wakes up, and cracks reappear on the mirror. This time they don't fade; they spread like spiderwebs across the entire glass. When she regains her senses, the cracks are gone, but in the next second, she clearly sees the other side of the mirror. "How did you get over there?"

Xiao Yu screams in panic upon seeing her. A woman walks in, her face identical to Yun's. She sees Yun in the mirror but doesn't scream, only masking her fear: "Don't close the door, no, no, where are you people?"

His son is right within reach, yet he cannot touch him. He lunges at the surface, but it is cold and hard. He rushes to the hallway, but it's empty. He calls his daughter in a frenzy. "I saw Xiao Yu; he looks exactly the same." His daughter just thinks he's had a nightmare.

At that moment, a thick fog rises outside the window, seeping through the walls and swallowing the whole house. The call cuts off. When his daughter calls back, the phone is off, and outside the door is an entirely unfamiliar world. The villa is gone. It's cold, and he's only wearing a thin shirt. In the alley behind the building, he pulls on a stranger's coat and walks toward the direction of his home. Looking back, the mirror is shattered.

He walks through the familiar tunnel, but where his home should be at the end, there is nothing. His phone has no signal, his throat burns, and the air quality index board at the convenience store reads 279. This isn't fog; it's lethal smog.

At the convenience store, he bumps into a girl who looks like his daughter. Closer inspection shows it's not Xiao Ye, but the boy with her recognizes him. "I'm heading back."

"Oh. Hey."

Xiao Qi doesn't know Xiao Ye, but he knows Xiao Yu—only here, he is called An'an. He's studying in the city; tomorrow after school, Xiao Qi is going to pick him up. He suppresses his racing heart and asks a few more questions.

This world is shrouded in perpetual smog; you must wear a mask to go out. The "he" here is called Meilin, a librarian, and his son is still alive, just raised as someone else's child.

In the original world, Xiao Ye can't reach her mother, so she asks Xiao Qi to check on her. Xiao Qi searches. Xiao Ye breathes a sigh of relief, but who exactly was that shadow? He catches sight of himself between the bookshelves in the city library. Here. At this moment, he is finally certain that he never went insane. He raises his phone, wanting to capture evidence. Meilin follows the light, catching sight of a familiar-looking figure, and calls out to him. Yun seizes the chance to escape, but leaves his phone behind in the library.

He doesn't look back, but heads straight for An'an's school; he must get to his son before the other version of himself does.

The school bell rings, and An'an comes out holding his teacher's hand. Sister Yun rushes over, but the child collapses right in front of her. "What's wrong? What's wrong? What's wrong with An'an? What's wrong with him? An'an, breathe, An'an. Mommy, breathe. An'an's mom, please help me with this."

The An'an here has epilepsy; the teacher skillfully calms him down. Sister Yun is stunned for a long moment before she finally pulls him into her arms. She takes An'an to the art studio to see if he remembers how to draw, but the studio has become a clinic, and someone calls out to her from behind.

"Hey, okay." It's her psychiatrist, Dr. An, but the look in this Dr. An's eyes hides hostility. A man walks out of the clinic—it's Lao Sun. Here, he isn't her brother-in-law, but An'an's attending physician. He insists on driving them home; Sister Yun wants to refuse, but An'an has already nodded.

In the library, Meilin finds that phone and casually puts it in the lost and found. A casual remark makes Meilin pause; that phone is exactly like hers. Recalling. The moment she unlocks the screen, she sees a photo of another version of herself and her son. She calls the school, but her son has already been picked up.

In the original world, Xiao Ye rushes home overnight, but his mother is nowhere to be found in any of the rooms. He hears a noise in the basement, goes in, and looks around, but it's empty. He doesn't notice a man crouching in the shadows. After he leaves, a crack appears on the basement mirror.

Lao Sun drops them off at the old house. Before she gets out, he calls out to Sister Yun, his words laced with hidden meaning. "Mom, the fog is coming, hurry inside."

The fog alarm sounds, and Sister Yun takes the chance to slip away. The house is in chaos, and the photo only shows Meilin and An'an. An'an starts to... She looks back, and outside the window, it's back to her own courtyard. The time for the passage is up. She tries to take An'an, but he pulls his hand away.

"Let's go, come on."

"Let's go, hurry."

"No, hurry, let's go, An'an, hurry."

"No, I don't want to."

"Hurry."

Opening her eyes again, she is back in the villa. She desperately tells her daughter and husband about that world, but no one believes her. Lao He is upset that his wife kept her phone off all day, causing their daughter, who is about to take the college entrance exam, to miss her classes. He decides to send her to Taipei for hospitalization. Sister Yun pulls a student ID from her coat pocket; it is the ID from the other world. But in Lao He's eyes, this is just proof that she is getting sicker. He tells her to pack; they leave tomorrow.

Sometimes the deepest loneliness isn't walking through the fog alone; it's seeing the truth clearly, but having no one willing to take a look with you.

At the hospital, after hearing her account, Dr. An didn't refute it and accepted it all; yet, this indiscriminate belief was more embarrassing to Sister Yun than suspicion. He got up to leave, as Lao He suspected that side effects of the medication were causing his wife to sleepwalk.

"One of the pills you prescribed her is Steelux. Its side effect is sleepwalking. Is it possible that what happened last night was just a side effect of the medication?"

"She took that pill before and told me it helped her sleep very well. If someone were sleepwalking, they wouldn't know what they were doing. She just described her entire experience very clearly, so it wasn't a dream."

Dr. An dismissed this theory; people who sleepwalk don't remember details, but Sister Yun remembers every single one. She seems trapped in an obsession, and Dr. An urged Lao He not to deny her, but to support her. "I believe the key to her recovery is you. I will continue to prescribe her medication, but these must be taken for over 7 days to be effective. Can you communicate with her properly? I will still arrange therapy for her."

But Sister Yun's obsession has already shifted in another direction; she wants to bring the other world... Lao He met with Lao Sun to talk about selling the house, but Lao Sun said his brother had cancelled the deal in person yesterday. But Lao He had been in meetings all day yesterday; he didn't dwell on it and just told Lao Sun to bring potential buyers as soon as possible.

Returning home, he pulled open the bedroom wardrobe, and inside stood another... Him. He grabbed a desk lamp but was no match, getting knocked out by a blow and dragged to the other side of the mirror. Meilin was stunned to see her husband carrying back another husband.

"Get out of the way!"

"Hurry up and help! What are you still standing there for? Hurry, hold his head down! Oh."

"Didn't you say you were going to tie up that woman first? Why did you bring him here instead?"

"What if someone finds me? What should I do? Come over and help!"

"How am I supposed to help?"

The truth finally surfaced from the depths. Meilin's husband, Lao Xu, had long discovered this passage. He saw another version of himself living in a villa with immense wealth, saw his other wife being gentle and his children being respectable, while he only had a drafty old house and a lifetime of poverty. So, he decided to try a different way of living—to swap places with the other version of himself.

He entered the villa and secretly observed the family; the figure Xiao Qi saw that night was him. Today, he had intended to kidnap Sister Yun first, but he ran into Lao Xu, who had come home early. He put on Lao He's clothes, mimicked his manner of speaking, and handed over the wedding ring that Sister Yun had lost long ago, which he'd fished out from the sink.

Sister Yun was pleasantly surprised, but noticed a few red spots on his arm. Lao Xu claimed they were bug bites, and she didn't think much of it. At dinner, she brought up Xiao Yu again, and this time, her husband believed every word. For the first time in 3 years, someone was on her side. She revealed all the clues: 2:30 AM, when the forbidden mirror opens, is the moment the portal activates, and she has to go. He said he would go with her.

She didn't know this gentleness was a trap. Lao Sun arrived with a contract; a big client was offering a high price. Lao Xu urged Sister Yun to sign it. Fearing he'd be exposed, he made an excuse that Sister Yun wasn't feeling well and asked Lao Sun to wait a few days.

In the mirror, Lao He woke up, his hands and feet bound with tape, with his wife sitting beside him.

"Ah, I just saw one... I grew one."

"Help me untie this."

"Hurry, help me untie it!"

"Don't move, don't move, just don't move."

"What on earth is Li Yun doing?"

"I am not Li Yun."

"Is that my husband?"

He thought his wife was ill and had developed a split personality, and that the man who looked just like him was merely an actor he had hired. He tore off the tape, cut his bindings, and pushed open the door. Outside the door was the gray, hazy old street. An'an, coming home from school, threw himself at his dad. "Dad, look at you today..."

It was Xiao Yu's face. Lao He finally understood that everything his wife had said was true. As the alarm sounded, Meilin pulled him back into the room; there were some strange marks left on the bathroom mirror. "Meilin." She took a tracksuit out of the closet; it was the one Lao He had lost months ago, and he had wrongly blamed his wife for it. It turned out their family had been watched inch by inch for a long time.

Meilin finally softened and revealed the origin of the portal.

She said, "5 months ago, when we renovated the basement bathroom, he saw you for the first time in the middle of the night. He said you were crying."

5 months ago, during the basement renovation, Lao Xu discovered the mirror was cracked while installing it. In the crack, another version of himself was hiding and crying secretly. From then on, Lao Xu began traveling back and forth between the 2 worlds. Meilin only knew that the mirror could allow one to see the people on the other side, but she didn't know... She said her husband just didn't want him to set his sights on An'an. But she clutched the wedding ring Lao Xu had left behind. For a man who left so decisively as to even abandon his wedding ring, Meilin began to feel a chill in her heart.

Lao Xu opened Lao He's account and was stunned—hundreds of millions! He felt that he had finally turned his life around. The next day, he accompanied Sister Yun to have dinner at his in-laws', but as soon as he saw the old people, he went stiff with fear. In his world, his father was an abuser, leaving scars on his mother for life. Now, sitting across from him is his father from another world, kind-faced, yet he dares not say a word. Yun notices his strange behavior. Xian Jiancheng.

"What's wrong?"

"Feeling."

The Lao Xu of this world was obsessed with risky bets and blew all their savings, ending up with worthless inventory. It was Meilin who cleaned up the mess bit by bit to keep the family afloat. The burns on the elder's body were left by Lao Xu's father. The more Lao Xu hears, the heavier his heart gets; to think the same face could lead such a miserable life.

An'an plays catch with him; the way he smiles is exactly like Xiao Yu. The alarm sounds outside again. Smog has reached level 6. Meilin stares at the TV, muttering that "this world can't hold on much longer."

A forbidden screen creates 2 lives: one of luxury and comfort, the other of barely surviving. But the cruelest part of this show is that no one on either side is truly happy. In wealthy homes, there are those trapped in the dark nights of losing a child; in old, poor tenements, there are those trapped in a lifetime of resentment. The so-called parallel world is never another possibility, but the rift in our hearts we dare not face; it has always been there, we just chose to cover the mirror.
