## 简介

动画电影《八仙》以吕洞宾寻回琉璃灯、联合钟离权等八仙盗宝为主线，在接连反转中揭开“无心苍”的真实身份与钟离权被逐师门的往事。影片借“苍去心为吕”的字谜和众人“为何而偷”的对照，讨论私欲、规则与牺牲：为钱可悲，为亲可怕，为民受罚。最终，八仙放下旧怨、救回众生，也完成钟离权的自我和解。

The phenomenal animated film "Eight Immortals" is finally here. It took in 2 billion at the summer box office, becoming the 3rd-highest-grossing domestic animated film, with netizens even saying that seeing this movie gives them hope for the future of Chinese animation.

The story takes place in a fictional era where a catastrophe occurs every 1,000 years. The Glazed Lamp, the treasure protecting humanity, was stolen, and it is the key to maintaining peace in the mortal realm. Lu Dongbin, still a mere mortal, travels alone to the legendary Penglai Immortal Island to find the Glazed Lamp. His first goal is to find his senior brother, Zhongli Quan, who had been expelled from their sect.

After much inquiry, he waits at a spot often frequented by Zhongli Quan on Penglai, but in a moment of distraction, his belongings are swiped by an unremarkable middle-aged man. Fortunately, Lu Dongbin has excellent reflexes and quickly catches the thief, only to discover after a brief struggle that the man is actually Zhongli Quan, the person he was looking for. He now goes by the name Han Zhongli, living a muddled life on Penglai every day.

Lu Dongbin states his purpose: the Glazed Lamp was stolen by the Three Stars of Fortune, Prosperity, and Longevity on Penglai Island and is now in their treasure vault, though it has been reforged by someone they hired. The only person who knows what the Glazed Lamp currently looks like is Zhongli Quan, because he was the one who helped the Three Stars reshape it, a grave crime in the eyes of the Heavenly Court. Zhongli Quan initially thinks Lu Dongbin is a plant trying to trap him and has no intention of cooperating, but he soon realizes the catch—besides the Three Stars, the only one who knows the lamp's location is the guardian deity previously responsible for it. This means Lu Dongbin is the guardian who lost the lamp back then, was banished to the mortal realm, and now wants to retrieve it to redeem himself. With this leverage, the two instantly agree to team up and sneak into the treasure vault. Lu Dongbin gets the lamp, Zhongli Quan gets the money, and they both have a bright future ahead.

However, a serious problem lies before them: to enter the vault, they need the key from the guard, but to get the key, they need the Golden Scissors to cut the magic rope tied to it, and those scissors are inside the vault—a perfect logical loop. Realizing this path is blocked, Zhongli Quan changes his strategy to use a Taoist temple near the vault and dig a tunnel from within it, leading directly into the treasure vault.

To take over the temple, the two must pose as immortals and go through the application process first. Zhongli Quan has a friend in the government, Royal Brother-in-law Cao, the Empress's brother. Hoping to use this connection to secure the Taoist temple, he discovers it has already been rented out. Cao suggests they borrow 2 sets of official robes to pose as government officials and evict the current tenants, but they are caught in the act while changing. It turns out the ones who rented the temple are Han Xiangzi and Zhang Guolao, experts in extortion and scams, respectively; they easily see through Zhongli Quan's petty tricks.

"It's useless. Can't you tell? They are not simple people."

"Why would you say they are 'not simple'?"

With forceful methods failing, Zhongli Quan has no choice but to disguise himself as Wuxin Cang, the deity Han Xiangzi worships, and reveal his plan to steal from the Treasure Pavilion. The four gather together, and just like that, the treasure-hunting team adds 2 more members as they discuss how to divide the work. Suddenly, a visitor to the temple makes a wish. If they don't handle it in time, they might be reported, leading to the temple being sealed off and ruining their entire plan. They are forced to take the mission to investigate the haunting in the temple, only to find it's just Old Li playing tricks.

"The master of the hall, right?"

"Yes, yes, yes. What a coincidence to run into you here, master."

Tieguai Li, knowing Zhongli Quan wouldn't move without profit, threatens to expose his true identity, forcing him to reveal the plan to steal from the Treasure Pavilion. And so, Tieguai Li and Lan Caihe also join the treasure-hunting team. However, they don't know that the mastermind behind the theft of the Glazed Lamp is someone else, someone even the Three Stars of Fortune, Prosperity, and Longevity treat with great respect.

Just as they are discussing how to use the God of Longevity's birthday banquet in 10 days to gather all the immortals, a loud noise suddenly comes from below. That's right, the Eight Immortals are digging a tunnel. The Three Stars naturally won't let such an annoying noise ruin the upcoming gathering, so they order the nearby temple to be razed before the banquet. Cao can only try to delay them, meaning the team must speed up and dig through to the Treasure Pavilion within 10 days.

As if that wasn't bad enough, because they had fulfilled wishes too quickly before, the reputation of the Eight Immortals spreads among the people. Over time, they become known as the "Eight Immortals Who Catch Pickpockets," and visitors flock to them to make wishes. With an endless stream of visitors, Zhongli Quan has to put down his work and temporarily go help them catch thieves. They intended to just wing it, but unexpectedly caught every thief they targeted. The Taoist temple's business exploded, and they made a small fortune out of nowhere. However, Zhongli Quan still wasn't satisfied. His eyes remained on the treasure in the vault, never expecting the Three Stars to bring the treasure to him.

It turned out the Three Stars had received a challenge from the master thief Wuxin, announcing he would steal the Glazed Lamp during the banquet. The trio panicked immediately. Coincidentally, they heard about the Eight Immortals' legendary skill in catching thieves and personally brought a large amount of treasure to beg for their help. At this moment, the tunnel the Immortals had dug hadn't been filled yet. Everyone froze, terrified that gold might fall into the hole and draw the Three Stars' attention. The Three Stars mistakenly assumed they were heroes who viewed money as dirt, trusting them even more. They even brought out the vault key, promising the treasure would be theirs if they protected the lamp.

This was like a windfall for Zhongli Quan and his group. However, Lu Dongbin didn't want the gold; he wanted the Glazed Lamp, which caused a disagreement. In a fit of rage, Lu Dongbin snatched the key. He was the most powerful fighter among them; the others combined were no match for him, so they couldn't take it back by force. Zhongli Quan devised a devious plan. Under the guise of brotherly bonding, he drugged Lu Dongbin's wine, and the unsuspecting Lu passed out after one sip. Zhongli Quan thought getting the key meant everything was settled, but while Lu was unconscious, a mysterious woman arrived, openly seeking the Glazed Lamp. Even with their combined strength, they were no match for her. It turned out she was He Xiangu, and she claimed to be the real Five Stars Chang.

Just as the group was being beaten back, Lu Dongbin finally stepped in. After just 2 exchanges, He Xiangu realized his exceptional skill and dared not continue. She chose to retreat immediately, unaware that if she had attacked just one more time, Lu Dongbin wouldn't have been able to hold on.

Afterward, Zhongli Quan apologized to Lu Dongbin for drugging him and revealed the story of his past years. He explained that he was expelled from his sect because he stole his master's Guanyin Nectar to save the drought-stricken world, only to be reported by his own junior brother and cursed by his master to be a thief forever. At first, Zhongli Quan refused to give in, vowing not to resort to thievery even if he were starving. Yet those he had once helped not only failed to offer a hand, but instead turned on him with vicious words, which made Zhong realize the true cruelty of the human heart for the first time. He abandoned his former naive ideals and thus spent his entire life in Penglai as a thief. He thought his story would convince Lu Dongbin to give up on saving the world, but he did not expect Lu to be completely different from his previous acquaintances; Lu still believed that what Zhong had done back then was wrong, and that saving the common people was never a mistake.

From then on, the group's goal was unified: to snatch the Glazed Lamp before He Xiangu, and to secretly make off with the Three Stars' treasures as well. On the day of the banquet, the immortals gathered. Maitreya Buddha possessed a magical artifact called the Qiankun Pouch, capable of holding everything in heaven and earth, so holding the Three Stars' treasures would be a simple task. Tieguai Li and Lan Caihe were responsible for the Qiankun Pouch, Han Xiangzi and Cao Guojiu were tasked with monitoring the venue, Zhang Guolao was in charge of the retreat, while Lu Dongbin and Zhongli Quan were to enter the Treasure Pavilion to steal the Glazed Lamp.

As it turned out, Lu Dongbin was forcibly intercepted by Sun Wukong, who wanted him to drink, leaving Zhongli Quan to head to the Treasure Pavilion alone. Fortunately, Lu Dongbin caught up quickly. The two found the Glazed Lamp disguised as a tribute, but just as they were about to retreat, they were ambushed by the trailing He Xiangu, who sucked them both into her Purple Gold Gourd. Just as the two were at a loss, the world suddenly spun, and they were released. It turned out that Tieguai Li and the others had stolen the Qiankun Pouch and promptly trapped He Xiangu inside it.

Now everything was set; they only needed to stash the money into the pouch and escape. However, upon seeing the banknotes on the ground, Zhongli Quan broke out in a cold sweat, for this money came from Fairy Yunhua of the Douniu Palace, who was Yang Jian's mother. Years ago, when Yang Jian failed to save his mother from Mount Peach, only a sliver of her spirit remained. He intended to use the Glazed Lamp to siphon the power of the celestial gods and use this money as a medium to extract mortal souls to resurrect her.

Before the group could react, Yang Jian activated the formation. All the immortals present were pinned to the ground by a divine force, their energy being slowly drained into the formation. Zhongli Quan and the others tried to slip away with the Glazed Lamp, but they ran right into Yang Jian. Zhong initially tried to bluff his way through, but Yang's "Heavenly Eye" could see through his entire life in an instant, and the fake tribute naturally could not escape his gaze. Yang didn't force his hand, but instead told him a secret he didn't even know himself: the person who betrayed him back then was actually the Treasure Guardian. Zhongli Quan never expected the man who ruined his life to be standing right before him, and he immediately clashed with Lu Dongbin.

Meanwhile, Yang Jian seized the chance to place the Glazed Lamp into the magic circle, and in an instant, the entire Treasure Pavilion began to collapse. Yang Jian scattered coins engraved with magic seals all over Penglai, and everyone who touched them had their souls extracted. They fled all the way to the beach outside the city. Looking back, the whole of Penglai was shrouded in human souls; there's no telling how many lives were lost.

In the end, Zhongli Quan decided to put past grudges aside to stop Yang Jian alongside Lu Dongbin. He Xiangu also chose to join them, and the Eight Immortals were officially reunited. However, they quickly encountered a serious problem: none of them could swim, so they couldn't cross the sea. Just then, Zhang Guolao's donkey did not merely walk into the sea; it suddenly transformed into its true form. It turned out to have the bloodline of a Pegasus. Its father was a Pegasus that escaped when Sun Wukong wreaked havoc in Heaven, and it was later born to a mortal donkey. Although the journey was convoluted, the group finally had a means of transport and quickly caught up with Yang Jian's airship. But being mere mortals, they were no match for Yang Jian.

Fortunately, Lu Dongbin still had one last ace up his sleeve. While drinking with Sun Wukong earlier, the intoxicated Great Sage gave him a tuft of hair and promised that if Lu Dongbin ever faced trouble, he just had to blow on the hair and he would arrive immediately. With the Great Sage as his backing, Zhongli Quan suddenly grew bold. No longer hiding, he led the group to challenge Yang Jian head-on.

"Take a look at this! As soon as I blow on this hair, the person you fear most in this life will appear right away."

"Time to finish him off!"

"Yang..."

"...you..."

Nothing is scarier than a sudden silence. Lu Dongbin suddenly remembered that the Great Sage might still be hungover. They had no choice but to bite the bullet and engage Yang Jian in a fierce battle using the magic treasures they had scavenged from the Pavilion. Eventually, they distracted Yang Jian through a coordinated attack and used Han Xiangzi's paper flute to summon lightning, successfully trapping Yang Jian inside the Qiankun Pouch. Just when they thought everything was settled, they realized even the Qiankun Pouch couldn't hold Yang Jian, as the beam from his Third Eye instantly tore a hole through it. By then, it was too late for the Eight Immortals to escape, and they were all wiped out by Yang Jian in an instant.

However, when Yang Jian returned to his throne and prepared to activate the Glazed Lamp, he discovered that it had already been swapped. It turns out the hair Zhongli Quan blew didn't summon the Great Sage, but rather 8 monkey descendants. They transformed into the Eight Immortals to stall Yang, while the real Eight Immortals took the opportunity to steal the lamp. By the time Yang tried to pursue them, it was too late. The Great Sage had arrived with heavenly troops to surround him, and ultimately, Yang Jian had to surrender.

On the other side, the Eight Immortals released the souls from the Glazed Lamp. Having fulfilled their merit, they prepared to part ways. Before leaving, Lu Dongbin took the lamp and gave his own sword to Zhongli Quan. This sword is meant only for the sect's finest disciple, yet Lu Dongbin told him that the sword had always belonged to him. Confused, Zhongli Quan returned to the sect, where he finally learned the truth from his master: "Cang Wuxin" was a pseudonym, and Lu Dongbin was the true Wuxin Cang.

Back then, Zhongli Quan used Guanyin's nectar to save the world, which also saved Lu Dongbin. From that day on, he followed in Zhongli Quan's footsteps, entering Zhongnan Mountain to cultivate immortality. After witnessing Zhongli Quan being demoted to a mortal and forced to live as a thief, Lu Dongbin grew completely disillusioned with the sect. He openly defied the sect's orders and suffered the same fate as Zhongli Quan, but he didn't fall into despair; instead, he chose to become a righteous "Wuxin Cang" who saves the world. The Fairy (He Xiangu) had also received his kindness, which is why she, too, wanted to become "Wuxin." The disciple who betrayed Zhongli Quan had indeed become a guardian deity, but Lu Dongbin captured him and used his identity to get close to Zhongli Quan, allowing them to steal the lamp and help Zhongli Quan regain his spirit. Before leaving, the master had given that sword to Lu Dongbin, hoping he would personally hand it over to Zhongli Quan.

"Go and bring him back. If he asks, tell him it came from me."

Thus, Lu Dongbin laid out his plan step by step, leading Zhongli Quan to join in unknowingly, with the ultimate goal of bringing him back to Zhongnan Mountain. At this moment, Zhongli Quan finally let go of his inner burden. He walked into the rain to express his gratitude to Lu Dongbin, who was far away on the horizon.

"The Eight Immortals," directed by Mou Zhenyang, was released on July 18, 2026. By National Day, its screening share had dropped to 4.6%, yet it remained the only film that day with an occupancy rate over 10%. After 2 extensions, it held on from the summer season until November, taking 77 days to surpass 2 billion in box office revenue. What's more interesting is that this was director Mou Zhenyang's first animated feature film—a newcomer leading a team of post-90s-generation creators to tell the story of small figures who had not yet attained immortality.

Towards the end, the film presents a riddle: the character "Cang" (蒼) without a "heart" (心) becomes "Lu" (呂). Many simply see it as a clever plot device to reveal an identity, but I believe the true message of the entire film is hidden within these characters. "Cang" consists of 2 "sun" (日) characters stacked together; remove the horizontal stroke from each, and you are left with 2 "mouth" (口) characters, which form "Lu" (呂). The horizontal stroke removed is the "heart" (心). A "heart" (心) is a box with something inside. The frame represents status, titles, and rules, while the core is what you personally desire. Only by removing that heart to leave an empty space can this person become Lu Dongbin. Therefore, the name "Wu Xin" (Heartless) never meant that this thief was cold-blooded; rather, it meant that when he stole, he never once had himself in mind.

If you think about it, almost everyone in this film steals. The Fu Beast and the Three Stars steal the lantern for their own wealth; Yang Jian steals the gods' powers and mortal souls to save his mother; Zhongli Quan stole the nectar back then for the people of the Han Dynasty. With the word "steal," the film doesn't draw a line between those who steal and those who don't, but rather "why they steal." Stealing for money is pathetic, stealing for kin is terrifying, and stealing for the common people is punished the most severely.

The most intriguing character is Yang Jian. Splitting the mountain to save one's mother is traditionally a myth of filial piety, but the film makes him the ultimate antagonist. He isn't greedy for money or power; he just loves someone too much. Yet this very love allows him to self-righteously treat an entire city of people as mere collateral, which is the most dangerous aspect of having a "heart." Once selfish motives are cloaked in the guise of family or justice, even the person themselves begins to believe they are in the right.

There is a detail in the film easily overlooked: when the master hands the sword to Lu Dongbin, he only says, "If he asks, just say it was given by me." This sword is only passed to the sect's most outstanding disciple. In other words, the master who once personally struck him actually knew all along who was right and who was wrong, yet he did nothing. He chose to uphold the rules, then folded his guilt into a sword, asking someone else to deliver it quietly. This is more unsettling than any villain; bad guys at least dare to admit what they want, while "good" men like him know right from wrong but only dare to offer compensation where no one can see.

Over 2,000 years ago, Zhuangzi wrote: "He who steals a hook is executed, while he who steals a state becomes a feudal lord." The Eight Immortals place this age-old dilemma before us once again: the one who steals a lamp is branded a thief, while the one who sets the rules... well... leaves a mark on our conscience.
